Preventive Maintenance for Industrial Electrical Networks in Ecuador: Complete 2026 Guide

This guide explains what a properly executed preventive maintenance plan must include, how often to schedule it, and what it costs.

Why is Preventive Electrical Maintenance Mandatory in Ecuador?

It's not just a best practice — in Ecuador it's a legal requirement and a contractual condition imposed by industrial insurers. Ecuador's Electrical Standard (NEC) establishes that owners of industrial electrical installations are responsible for keeping them in safe and operational condition. The ARCERNNR (Ecuador's electricity regulatory agency) can order suspension of service to any installation that poses a risk.

Beyond the regulatory framework, the practical consequences of neglecting your plant's electrical network are severe:

  • Fires caused by faulty connections, overheated conductors, or electrical arcs
  • Progressive damage to production equipment from undetected voltage fluctuations
  • Unplanned production line shutdowns, often at the worst possible moment
  • Fines and suspension of operating permits for regulatory non-compliance
  • Voided insurance policies due to lack of documented maintenance records

The NEC does not set a single schedule for every industry, but in practice a documented maintenance plan is the evidence requested by:

  • The Fire Department when renewing the operating permit
  • Insurers, to keep fire policies active and free of exclusions
  • Auditors from corporate clients that require NEC/IEC compliance from their suppliers
Key figure: A 4-hour unplanned outage at a mid-sized industrial plant in northern Ecuador can represent losses from USD 3,000 depending on the industry. An annual preventive maintenance contract costs from USD 800 depending on scope. The math speaks for itself.

What Does an Industrial Electrical Maintenance Plan Include?

A well-structured preventive maintenance plan is far more than "checking the cables." It involves specific technical activities using calibrated instruments and documented results. Here are the essential components:

Infrared Thermographic Inspection

Infrared thermography is the most effective tool in modern electrical maintenance. It detects hot spots in switchboards, connections, conductors, and equipment before they cause failures. A loose connection terminal can reach 80°C while the rest of the circuit is at 30°C — invisible to the naked eye, perfectly identifiable with thermography.

The thermographic report must identify each anomaly, classify it by severity (minor, moderate, critical), and recommend an intervention timeline. It is a document accepted by insurers and regulatory authorities.

Switchboard and Substation Maintenance

Electrical switchboards are the heart of power distribution in any plant. Maintenance includes:

  • Interior cleaning by vacuuming and dry compressed-air blowing
  • Tightening and verification of all connections (terminals, busbars, breakers)
  • Visual inspection of conductors, insulation, and cable trays
  • Operational check of thermal-magnetic and molded-case circuit breakers
  • Review of protection relays and setting calibration
  • For substations: transformer inspection (oil level, temperature, deformation), cell cleaning, and disconnector switch verification

Insulation Resistance Testing

A megohmmeter (Megger) measures the insulation resistance of conductors, motors, and transformers. Degraded insulation is a failure waiting to happen. IEC 60364 establishes minimum insulation values based on voltage level. Motors with insulation below 1 MΩ must be serviced before returning to operation.

Grounding System Verification

The grounding system is the most critical electrical safety system in any industrial installation. Measurement is performed with an earth tester using the three-point fall-of-potential method. High resistance values indicate degraded electrodes, corroded joints, or overly dry soil with poor conductivity.

Warning: A deficient grounding system doesn't just create electrocution risk for personnel — it can generate dangerous step voltages throughout the plant area and cause stray-current damage to electronic equipment and motors. Never skip this verification.

Protection Relay Calibration

Protection relays (overcurrent, earth fault, differential) and molded-case breakers must be periodically verified to confirm they operate at the time and current for which they were set. A relay that fails to trip on time turns a manageable fault into a destructive short circuit or a fire.

What to Demand from Your Electrical Maintenance Provider

Not all electrical maintenance services are equal. Before signing a contract, verify the following:

  • Certified engineers: Staff must hold an Electrical Engineering degree or equivalent and be registered with SENESCYT
  • Calibrated instruments: Thermal camera, megohmmeter, earth tester, and power quality analyzer — all with valid calibration certificates
  • Written technical reports: Every visit must produce a report with findings, measurements, photographs, and recommendations. No report, no real maintenance
  • Regulatory compliance: The provider must know and apply Ecuador's NEC, INEN standards, and current ARCERNNR regulations
  • Liability insurance: Essential for work on active industrial installations
  • Documented plan: The contract must specify which activities are performed, at what frequency, and with which instruments
Best practice: Require that each maintenance report includes a comparison table with values measured in the previous visit. This lets you spot trends: if a connection's temperature rises 10°C per semester, that's an early warning before failure. The maintenance log is also your strongest defense with insurers and regulatory authorities.
