Website Loading Speed: How Ecuadorian Businesses Can Improve It in 2026

If your site takes more than three seconds to load on a phone, you lose customers before they read your offer. What Google measures, what slows you down, and how to fix it.

A potential customer opens your site on their phone, stares at a blank screen for four seconds, and leaves for your competitor. You never find out. If your company invested in a website and inquiries aren't coming in, the website loading speed is the first place to look.

Why loading speed decides whether people contact you

Google has published that the chance of a mobile visitor leaving a page rises sharply as load time goes from one to three seconds. Since 2021, page experience (Core Web Vitals) has also been a ranking signal. A slow site loses customers and loses positions in search results.

In Ecuador this matters even more: most visits come from mobile phones, often on 4G connections that vary by area. A site that loads well on office fiber can be unusable at a plant site or on the road.

Core Web Vitals: the three metrics Google measures

Google evaluates page experience with three indicators. These are the official thresholds for a "good" rating:

MetricWhat it measuresGood value
LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)How long the main content takes to appear2.5 s or less
INP (Interaction to Next Paint)How fast the page reacts to a tap or click200 ms or less
CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)How much content jumps around while loading0.1 or less
How to measure it for free: paste your URL into Google's PageSpeed Insights and check the mobile result, not just desktop. Search Console shows the same report for your whole site.

The most common causes of a slow website

Unoptimized images

This is cause number one. A 4 MB photo uploaded straight from a phone or camera can outweigh the rest of the page combined. The fix: modern formats (WebP or AVIF), sizes that match the screen, and lazy loading for images below the fold.

The wrong hosting

An overloaded shared server is slow before it sends the first byte. For a corporate site, static hosting with a CDN usually performs better, serving files from a node close to the visitor.

Heavy plugins, scripts and templates

Every WordPress plugin, chat widget, tracking pixel or extra font adds requests. A generic template ships code for dozens of features your company never uses. A custom-built site loads only what it needs.

Mistakes we see on Ecuadorian company websites

  • A background video on the homepage: it weighs tens of megabytes and almost nobody watches it fully on a phone.
  • Oversized catalog banners: product photos uploaded exactly as they came out of the camera.
  • Embedded social feeds and maps that load before the main content.
  • No HTTPS certificate, or mixed content, which the browser flags as not secure.
  • No owner: the site was published once and has never been measured again.

None of these is serious on its own. Together they explain why so many corporate sites score poorly on mobile even though they look fine on the office computer.

What to do: a five-step improvement plan

  1. Measure first. Record your mobile LCP, INP and CLS before changing anything.
  2. Compress your images and convert them to WebP.
  3. Remove what you don't use: plugins, third-party scripts and extra fonts.
  4. Enable caching and a CDN to serve files from a nearby location.
  5. Measure again and repeat every quarter as part of your website maintenance routine (article in Spanish).

When to rebuild instead of patching

If the site is still in the red on mobile after optimizing images and removing plugins, the problem is the foundation: template, hosting or architecture. In that case custom web development costs less than endless patching, and it pays for itself with the inquiries you stop losing.
