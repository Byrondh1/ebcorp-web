Preventive Electrical Maintenance for Industrial Plants in Ecuador

The most expensive production stoppages rarely give warning. How a preventive electrical maintenance plan prevents fires, fines, and losses in your plant.

What is industrial preventive electrical maintenance?

Preventive electrical maintenance is a set of scheduled technical reviews and tasks performed on fixed dates — without waiting for something to fail — to keep a plant's electrical installation safe and efficient. Unlike a one-time audit, preventive maintenance is a continuous cycle: panel cleaning, connection re-tightening, contactor lubrication, protection calibration, and grounding checks, repeated at intervals defined by the risk level of each circuit.

In Ecuador, where much of the industrial base still runs on panels and transformers over 10 years old, this kind of maintenance is the difference between a plant that runs without surprises and one facing unexpected outages every few months.

Preventive, corrective, and predictive: not the same thing

Corrective maintenance

Action is taken after the failure. It's the most expensive option: it combines repair cost with the cost of stopped production.

Preventive maintenance

Action is taken before the failure, on a fixed schedule, regardless of the equipment's actual condition at that moment. It drastically reduces the likelihood of unplanned downtime.

Predictive maintenance

Uses measurements (thermography, vibration analysis, power quality) to anticipate the exact moment a component will fail. It's the ideal complement to a preventive plan, not a replacement for it.

How often should industrial electrical maintenance be done?

Frequency depends on the type of installation and its load level. As a general reference for companies in Ecuador:

  • Main switchboards and substation — Semi-annual review, with annual thermography
  • Secondary distribution panels — Quarterly review
  • Distribution transformers — Annual check of oil, bushings, and connections
  • Grounding systems — Annual measurement with a ground resistance tester
  • UPS and generator sets — Quarterly maintenance (see also our guide on UPS and generators for businesses)
  • Motors and industrial lighting — Semi-annual review
Key fact: most electrical fires in industrial plants don't happen because of new installations, but because of connections nobody re-tightened for years. A preventive plan eliminates that blind spot.

What does a preventive maintenance plan include?

A serious electrical network maintenance plan isn't limited to "checking that everything turns on." Every visit must be documented with:

  1. Internal and external panel cleaning (dust, moisture, corrosion)
  2. Re-tightening of terminals and connections with a calibrated torque wrench
  3. Inspection of breakers, contactors, and protection relays
  4. Ground resistance measurement
  5. Inspection of wiring, conduits, and safety labeling
  6. Thermography of critical panels (associated predictive maintenance)
  7. Technical report with findings, photos, and action plan

This last point is the one most often skipped in Ecuador: without a documented report, there's no evidence of compliance during a NEC code audit or an insurance claim.

What happens if you skip preventive maintenance?

Real risks of postponing maintenance:
  • Unplanned downtime — Every hour of stopped production can cost thousands of dollars in lost output
  • Shortened equipment lifespan — Motors and transformers failing early due to accumulated overheating
  • Fines and non-compliance — Facilities unable to prove maintenance to the utility or to ARCSA/GMP regulators
  • Denied insurance claims — Without a maintenance log, insurers can deny coverage for an electrical incident
