How to Choose a Web Development Company in Ecuador: 7 Criteria and Red Flags

A badly hired website costs you twice: once to build it and again to redo it. These are the criteria that separate a serious web development company from an improvised one.

Many Ecuadorian companies hire their website from the first contact who replies, or from the lowest bidder, and months later discover the site is slow, invisible on Google, or nobody knows who holds the domain password. The way to avoid that is simple: choose the right web development company in Ecuador from day one.

The most common mistake: comparing on price alone

A $400 quote and a $1,500 quote can look identical on a PDF, but they are not. The difference is usually what you cannot see: clean code, speed, security, technical SEO and after-launch support. 7 criteria for choosing a web development company

1. A portfolio of real, verifiable projects

Ask for links to live sites, not just screenshots. Open them on your phone, check that they load fast and confirm that forms and contact buttons really work. If possible, write to one of their clients and ask how support was after delivery.

2. Experience in your industry

A team that understands industrial or commercial business knows which content, catalog and forms you need to attract B2B clients, and how to present certifications, projects and services credibly.

3. Technical SEO built in from the design stage

Titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, speed and structured data are not "added later"; they are built from the start.

4. Full ownership of your site

The domain, hosting and code must be in your name. If you do not have access, you do not own your website.

5. Responsive and accessible design

Most visits come from mobile. The site must work well on small screens and be usable with a keyboard.

6. A maintenance and support plan

A website needs updates, backups and monitoring. Know the cost and scope before signing; here is what website maintenance includes (Spanish).

7. Clear communication and written deadlines

Delivery dates, number of revisions and a project owner should all be in the contract.

WordPress or custom code: which fits your company

There is no single "best" technology for everyone. WordPress lets your team edit text without technical help, but it demands constant plugin and security updates. A custom site built in HTML, CSS and JavaScript loads faster, has fewer vulnerable points and usually costs less to maintain, although content changes go through the developer. A quality company will explain both options with their strengths and limits, and recommend the one that fits your operation, not the one that is most convenient for them.

Timelines and budget: what is reasonable

A corporate site of 5 to 8 pages usually takes 2 to 4 weeks if the client delivers the copy, logo and photographs on time. Be wary of anyone who promises a complete website in three days, or who cannot give you a date. On payment, the usual approach is milestones: a deposit at the start, a payment when the design is approved, and the balance at launch. This protects both sides and makes progress verifiable.

Also remember that a website does not end on delivery day: training on the admin panel, handover of all access credentials and a warranty for bugs during the first weeks should all be in writing.

Red flags before you hire

  • They promise "#1 on Google in 30 days".
  • They show no live projects or client references.
  • They use unmodified templates without saying so.
  • They keep control of your domain or hosting access.
  • They cannot explain what happens after delivery.
  • They ask for 100% of the payment upfront.

Questions to ask before signing

  1. Can I see three sites you built and talk to those clients?
  2. Who will own the domain, hosting and code?
  3. How will you measure the site's speed and search ranking?
  4. What does support cost, and what does it cover after delivery?
  5. How many design revisions are included?
