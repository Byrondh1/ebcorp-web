Preventive Electrical Maintenance for Industrial Plants in Ecuador: Full Plan and Costs

An unplanned electrical failure can shut down your plant for days. Learn what a preventive electrical maintenance plan includes, how often to run it, and what it costs in Ecuador.

What is preventive electrical maintenance?

Preventive electrical maintenance is a set of inspections, adjustments, and tests scheduled at fixed intervals, designed to catch and fix the wear of an electrical installation before it causes a failure. Instead of waiting for something to break and repairing it afterward, preventive maintenance targets the root cause: connections that loosen from vibration, insulation that degrades from heat, or protective devices that drift out of calibration over time.

Three approaches, don't mix them up: Corrective maintenance repairs after a failure — the most expensive option. Preventive maintenance intervenes on a fixed schedule, regardless of the equipment's actual condition. Predictive maintenance uses measurements like thermography and power quality analysis to intervene only when the data shows real risk. A solid plan combines preventive maintenance as the baseline with predictive checks to prioritize.

Why it's critical for your business

In an industrial plant, electrical power isn't just another utility — it's the condition for everything else to run. A single switchboard failure can stop entire production lines, and most of these failures give measurable warning signs weeks or months before they happen.

What skipping a maintenance plan actually costs:
  • Unplanned production downtime — Almost always more expensive than any annual maintenance plan
  • Higher energy costs — Loose connections, harmonics, and low power factor drive up billed consumption
  • Fire risk — Corroded or overloaded connections are the leading cause of electrical fires in industrial plants
  • Shorter equipment lifespan — Voltage fluctuations and phase imbalance accelerate wear on motors and machinery
  • Insurance and client audit issues — Missing maintenance records is a common reason insurers reject claims or auditors reject certifications

What a preventive electrical maintenance plan includes

A well-designed plan is more than a visual check. It combines inspection, measurement, and documentation on every visit:

1. Periodic visual and thermographic inspection

Switchboards, connections, and wiring are checked with a thermal camera, looking for abnormal heating points that reveal an impending failure before it becomes visible to the naked eye.

2. Connection tightening and cleaning

Connection screws loosen over time from vibration and heating-cooling cycles. Re-torquing to spec and cleaning off corrosion eliminates the most common failure point in industrial switchboards.

3. Protection and grounding tests

Breakers and relays are checked to confirm they trip within the correct time, and the grounding system's resistance is measured with a ground tester, in line with the limits set by Ecuador's NEC standard.

4. Power quality verification

Harmonics, power factor, and phase imbalance are measured. Correcting them early avoids utility penalties and extends the life of motors and drives.

5. Log and drawing updates

Every visit is documented: what was checked, what was fixed, and what needs follow-up. This history is essential for audits, insurance, and planning replacements before they fail.

How often should it be done?

Frequency depends on the component and the environment — dust, humidity, temperature, and how critical the process is:

Component Recommended frequency Main activity
Main switchboards Quarterly Thermography and connection tightening
Transformers Every 6 months Thermography and dielectric oil analysis
Motors and drives Every 6 months Vibration measurement and insulation resistance
Grounding system Yearly Resistance measurement with ground tester
UPS and generators Monthly / quarterly Start-up test and battery condition check
Emergency lighting Every 6 months Runtime test and battery replacement

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between preventive and predictive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance runs on a fixed schedule regardless of the equipment's actual condition. Predictive maintenance uses measurements — thermography, vibration, oil analysis — to intervene only when the data shows a failure trend. The strongest plans combine both.

How often should industrial electrical maintenance be done in Ecuador?

It depends on the component: main switchboards every 3 months, transformers and motors every 6 months, and grounding at least once a year. Dusty, humid, or corrosive environments should shorten these intervals.

Does preventive electrical maintenance require stopping production?

Most activities are done with the installation energized. Only connection tightening and some insulation tests require cutting a specific circuit, and those are scheduled zone by zone so the whole plant doesn't stop.

Is it required by any regulation in Ecuador?

Ecuador's NEC standard requires industrial electrical installations to maintain safe operating conditions, which in practice means documented periodic maintenance. Utilities and insurers also frequently request maintenance records.
