Industrial Electrical Maintenance in Ecuador: Service Plan and Costs (2026)

An electrical failure never gives you a warning. An overheated panel, a loose torque connection, or degraded insulation can shut down your production for hours or start a fire. This guide explains what a serious industrial electrical maintenance plan includes, how often it should run, and what it costs in Ecuador.

Why preventive electrical maintenance is non-negotiable

Most Ecuadorian companies only call an electrician once something has already failed: a breaker that trips, a motor that burns out, an outage that halts the production line. By then, the cost is no longer maintenance — it's emergency repair, lost production, and in the worst case, a fire.

An industrial electrical maintenance plan flips that logic: it inspects, measures, and corrects issues before the failure happens. It isn't an expense — it's insurance that pays for itself the first time it prevents an unplanned shutdown.

Real data: Up to 80% of industrial electrical failures originate from loose connections, overloads, and hot spots — problems that preventive maintenance with thermography detects months before they turn into a plant shutdown or a fire.

Corrective vs. preventive maintenance: the difference that costs your business

Many companies still operate under the "if it's not broken, don't touch it" model. The problem is that in industrial electrical systems, by the time something breaks, it's already too late.

Corrective maintenance

  • Action happens after the failure, with production already stopped
  • Emergency repairs cost 3-5× more than a scheduled visit
  • Real risk of fire or workplace accident
  • No traceability or history of the installation's condition

Preventive maintenance

  • Scheduled visits, no emergency surprises
  • Cuts unplanned failures by up to 70%
  • Logs and reports that support insurance and permits
  • Extends the service life of panels, motors, and transformers

Predictive maintenance: the next level

Predictive maintenance goes a step beyond a fixed calendar: it uses infrared thermography, vibration analysis, and insulation testing to gauge the real condition of each component before intervening. It's the same technology we use in our industrial electrical audits, applied periodically within a maintenance plan.

What a complete industrial electrical maintenance plan includes

A serious plan is not "check that everything still turns on." At minimum, it should cover:

  • Visual inspection and cleaning of distribution and control panels
  • Connection and busbar torque checks per manufacturer specification
  • Infrared thermography of panels, breakers, and critical connections
  • Ground resistance measurement
  • Protection device verification (breakers, relays, motor starters)
  • Power factor and power quality review
  • Technical report with findings, thermal images, and an action plan

How often should industrial electrical maintenance be scheduled

Not every component needs the same frequency. As a general reference for an industrial facility in Ecuador:

  • Visual inspection and panel cleaning: every 6 months
  • Infrared thermography: once a year (twice a year in critical environments)
  • Connection torque checks: annual
  • Grounding system: every 12–24 months
  • 24/7 facilities (hospitals, data centers, continuous plants): quarterly reviews
Best practice: Keep a digital log of every visit, with the date, findings, and actions taken. Beyond making tracking easier, it's the evidence insurers and fire departments ask for when renewing policies and operating permits.

Frequently asked questions

How often should industrial electrical maintenance be done?

It depends on the equipment, but as a general rule: visual inspection and cleaning of switchboards every 6 months, annual thermography, annual connection torque checks, and grounding tests every 12-24 months. Critical facilities (hospitals, data centers, 24/7 plants) need quarterly reviews.

What is the difference between preventive and corrective maintenance?

Corrective maintenance fixes a failure after it happens, almost always with production stopped and at a higher cost. Preventive maintenance inspects and corrects issues before they cause a failure, through scheduled visits, cutting unplanned downtime by up to 70%.

How much does an industrial electrical maintenance plan cost in Ecuador?

A basic semi-annual plan for a small business costs between $300 and $800 per visit. An intermediate plan with annual thermography runs $800 to $2,000. A full plan for large industrial plants, with quarterly visits and monitoring, can range from $3,000 to $10,000+ per year, depending on facility size.

Is electrical maintenance mandatory under Ecuadorian regulations?

The NEC standard doesn't mandate a single maintenance schedule, but it does require electrical installations to be kept in safe, operational condition. In practice, insurers and fire departments ask for maintenance records to renew policies and operating permits.
